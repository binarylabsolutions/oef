# Research: Pakistan's education context

*Compiled 30 Sep 2026. Every figure links to its source. Where two official sources disagree, both are shown.*

## Key takeaways for us

1. **The crisis is learning, not only enrolment.** About 25 million children are out of school, and 80% of children at late-primary age can't read with understanding.
2. **Short video is already the mass channel.** TikTok (79.9m) and YouTube (54.3m) reach far more Pakistanis than Instagram (22.4m). **Our content must be on TikTok, YouTube and Facebook, not only Instagram.**
3. **Women are underserved online:** 28.8% of social media users are women; 28% of women who use mobile internet do so only on someone else's phone.
4. **TV reached 95% of people during COVID vs 36% for the internet.** For the hardest-to-reach children, TV, radio and offline packs matter more than apps.
5. **Legal risk is real:** new "false information" offences under PECA 2025 and strict blasphemy laws mean accuracy review and a religion-neutral scope are essential, not optional.
6. **Many players do curriculum lessons; few do curiosity, documentary or creativity content.**

## Legal and regulatory

- **PECA 2016, s.37** lets the PTA block content on grounds including religion, security, public order and morality ([Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2266027/pta-can-block-or-remove-unlawful-online-content-on-social-media)).
- **PECA amendments 2025** added a social media regulatory authority and s.26A, up to 3 years' prison for spreading "false and fake information" ([Amnesty](https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2025/01/pakistan-authorities-pass-bill-with-sweeping-controls-on-social-media/)).
- **Blasphemy laws** (PPC 295-A to 298-C) carry penalties up to death ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blasphemy_in_Pakistan)).
- **TV** needs a PEMRA licence; web content falls under PTA rules ([IBA](https://www.ibanet.org/Media-and-telecommunications-frameworks-in-Pakistan)).
- **Registration:** a Section 42 company needs an SECP licence ([SECP](https://www.secp.gov.pk/document/guidelines-for-obtaining-license-under-section-42-of-the-companies-act-2017/)); trusts and societies are alternatives. Tax exemption under s.2(36) of the Income Tax Ordinance, with certification via the Pakistan Centre for Philanthropy ([PCP](https://pcp.org.pk/npo-certification-2/)).

## Curriculum and language

- National Curriculum 2022-23 covers Urdu, English, Maths, Science, Pakistaniat and religious education; provinces may adapt it; Sindh rejected the Single National Curriculum ([NCC](https://ncc.gov.pk/GoPInitiative), [Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2283681/sindh-deems-single-national-curriculum-infeasible)).
- Mother tongues: Punjabi 38.8%, Pashto 18.2%, Sindhi 14.6%, Saraiki 12.2%, Urdu 7.1% ([Friday Times](https://www.thefridaytimes.com/18-Aug-2025/how-language-policy-shapes-pakistan-s-unity-and-why-balance-matters)). Urdu is the shared language, but regional languages matter for the youngest and hardest to reach.
