# Content strategy

*Last updated: 30 Sep 2026 · Not final*

What we make, for whom, in what order, and what we deliberately don't make. Based on [10-learning-in-the-ai-era.md](10-learning-in-the-ai-era.md) and the [YouTube landscape research](research/education-youtube-landscape.md).

## Our lane

Pakistan already has large curriculum lesson libraries (Sabaq Foundation, Taleemabad). What it lacks is **Urdu curiosity content**: science edutainment, how-things-work, documentaries, creativity shows for children. That's where we lead. We link to the lesson libraries rather than duplicate them.

**Positioning:** *the curiosity studio of Pakistan*. We make people want to learn; lesson platforms help them study.

## Where we publish

TikTok reaches 79.9m Pakistanis, YouTube 54.3m, Facebook 52.9m and Instagram 22.4m ([research](research/pakistan-education-context.md)). Every creator we support posts short videos to **TikTok, YouTube Shorts and Facebook as well as Instagram**, and long videos to YouTube. WhatsApp channels carry daily questions and low-data versions.

## Language policy: Urdu first

Our content is for as many Pakistanis as possible, so Urdu, the language most of the country shares, is the default.

| Rule | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Every piece has an Urdu version** | No studio-supported content exists only in English or only in another language |
| **English words are fine** | Scientific and technical terms can stay in English, explained in Urdu the first time ("velocity, yaani raftaar aur simt") |
| **Other versions are welcome, in addition** | A creator can also publish English, Punjabi, Pashto, Sindhi, Saraiki or Balochi versions; the Urdu version comes first or at the same time |
| **"Urdu version" means Urdu audio** | Subtitles alone don't count. A re-recording or a dub (human, or AI-assisted and checked by a native speaker) is fine |
| **On-screen text** | Urdu script or simple Roman Urdu; English only for terms |
| **Plain Urdu** | Everyday spoken Urdu, not heavy literary Urdu, so that viewers whose first language is Punjabi, Pashto or Sindhi can follow |

- **English-first creators** (e.g. programming, O/A-level) are welcome if they add Urdu versions; the studio's dubbing and subtitle pipeline can help.
- **Regional-language creators** are welcome too, with the same rule: an Urdu version of every piece, which the studio can help make.
- Subtitles in English (and later regional languages) are added to Urdu videos where possible, for reach and accessibility.

## Who we make content for

We start broad and go deeper, so that ordinary people, not just students, can learn from us.

| Audience layer | Who | What they need from us | Share of output (Year 1) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Everyone** | Families, adults, anyone scrolling | Curiosity, everyday science, stories, myth-busting, judgement | 50% |
| **Young learners (6–14)** | Children in and out of school | Wonder, creativity, basic concepts, reading and maths joy; no reading required | 20% |
| **Students (14–20)** | Matric, intermediate, O/A level | Understanding behind the syllabus; how to learn; careers | 20% |
| **Teachers** | School and tuition teachers | How to teach a topic; analogies; demos; tools | 10% |

Hard skills (programming, advanced maths) are welcome, but they come through creators who specialise in them. The studio's own flagship content stays in the "everyone" and "young learners" layers, where the most people can benefit.

## Content scope

| Tier | Topics | Studio support |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Core** (we actively build) | Science and nature; maths and logic; how things work and technology; history of science and ideas; Pakistan's land, nature and heritage; critical thinking, media and AI literacy; creativity, art and making; life skills (health, money, civic sense, climate and disaster readiness); foundational reading and maths | Full: volunteers, experts, tools, promotion |
| **Supported** (creators welcome) | Programming and digital skills; languages; exam preparation; careers and freelancing; universal ethics and character | Full, if the creator follows the charter |
| **Out of scope** | Religious instruction, theology and sectarian topics; party politics and political commentary; "get rich" trading and financial tips; medical advice beyond established public-health guidance | None; we don't produce, edit or promote it |

## Religion: our position

**The studio is non-sectarian and does not produce religious instruction.** Creators are free to be personally religious; we simply don't make religious-education content.

Why:
- **Fairness.** Supporting one school of thought means excluding others; Pakistan has many, and we want every family to trust us.
- **Legal and safety risk.** Religious content can draw accusations under strict blasphemy laws, with severe consequences for creators and volunteers ([research](research/pakistan-education-context.md)).
- **Focus.** Religious education is already widely available through mosques, madrassas and many established creators.

What we *do* include, with care:
- History of science across civilisations, including the Muslim Golden Age (Ibn al-Haytham, Al-Khwarizmi, Al-Biruni), told as history of science.
- Ethics and character grounded in values shared across faiths: honesty, kindness, fairness, responsibility.
- Respect: we never mock or debate any faith, and we avoid "science vs religion" framing.

Religious-education creators may use our free, open tools and templates like anyone else, but studio volunteers don't work on religious content.

## Faceless and AI-made channels: our position

**We judge the content, not the face.** Some of the best education channels in the world are faceless (Kurzgesagt is an animation studio). Animated characters explaining cloud computing or physics can be excellent.

| We support faceless / AI-assisted creators who… | We don't support… |
| --- | --- |
| Have a named, accountable person behind the channel (it can be private to us) | Anonymous mass-produced channels |
| Have every script fact-checked by a human | Unreviewed AI scripts |
| Add original teaching value: their own explanations, structure, examples | Templated, repetitive content (YouTube already demonetises this as "inauthentic") |
| Disclose AI use as YouTube requires, and in our credit block | Deepfakes of real people, or cloned voices without consent |
| Follow the charter like everyone else | Content made to farm views rather than teach |

**Opportunity:** AI animation and voice tools make Urdu and regional-language cartoons affordable for the first time. For young learners and viewers who can't read, a well-made animated Urdu explainer may be the best format we have. We should build it ourselves, carefully.

## Priorities for Pakistan (first 3 years)

1. **Everyday science and how things work, in Urdu.** Proven by Rohail's numbers; the widest appeal.
2. **Judgement: critical thinking, misinformation and AI literacy.** Urgent, rarely taught, fits "Humans logic lagao".
3. **Curiosity and creativity for children.** Animated explainers, a making-and-art show, no reading needed.
4. **Pakistan's land, nature and heritage.** Documentary-style shorts first (the seed of the "National Geographic of Pakistan").
5. **Understanding behind the syllabus** for matric and intermediate students, linked to existing lesson libraries.
6. **Life skills:** health, money, civic sense, heat and flood readiness.
7. **Digital skills and careers**, through specialist creators.

## The flagship shows we're building towards

These are **studio-owned** series, made with the best volunteers and experts, in addition to what individual creators make.

| Show | Idea | Audience | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Curiosity show** (working title "Kyun?") | One big "why?" per episode, animated, in Urdu | Everyone, especially children | The Kurzgesagt/TED-Ed lane, which is missing in Urdu |
| **Making show** (working title "Bana Ke Dekho") | Art, craft and building from everyday and waste materials, hosted by a presenter kids can copy | Children 6–14 | Revives the arts-and-crafts TV show many Pakistanis grew up with; hands-on learning |
| **Pakistan documentary series** (working title "Hamara Pakistan") | Nature, geology, rivers, wildlife, engineering and heritage of Pakistan | Everyone | The start of the "National Geographic of Pakistan" |
| **National school quiz** | Schools compete on science and general knowledge | Students | Community and schools as partners; follows the Pakistani TV-quiz tradition |

## Quality and style rules

- One idea per short video; every claim sourced; human fact-check before publishing.
- Urdu first, simple words; English terms introduced with Urdu explanation (see the language policy above).
- Examples from Pakistani daily life: rickshaws, chai, load-shedding, cricket, weddings, monsoon.
- Every province represented over time; no single-city lens.
- Humour welcome, humiliation never (no mocking people who answered wrongly on the street).
- Every piece ends with a question, an action, or a next step on the ladder.
