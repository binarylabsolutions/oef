# Charter

*Last updated: 30 Sep 2026 · Not final*

Every creator, contributor and core-team member accepts this charter when they join. It is short on purpose: if a rule is not here, use the tenets to decide.

## The seven tenets

1. **Free forever.** Everything educational we help make is free to access for anyone, anywhere, forever. The studio never earns money from it.
2. **Credit everyone, and say what AI did.** Every person who worked on a piece is named, with their role, where the audience can see it; every part made or helped by AI is disclosed. (See [section 5](#5-ai-transparency).)
3. **Own our mistakes.** No claim goes out without a human fact-check, and when we still get something wrong, we say so in public, fix it, and thank whoever caught it. Being corrected is how science works; hiding a mistake is the only real failure. (See [section 4](#4-corrections-owning-our-mistakes).)
4. **Teach, don't sell.** Our spaces exist for education only. No selling, no promotions, no politics, no hate.
5. **Open by default.** Our templates, playbooks, tools and numbers are shared openly unless privacy requires otherwise.
6. **Free to leave.** No contracts, no exclusivity. Anyone can pause or leave at any time, and their credits stay.
7. **Respect people's time.** Small, clear tasks; honest deadlines; no guilt for pausing.

## 1. The free-forever rule

### What counts as "studio content"

Any education content that received studio help: a script reviewed, a video edited, a cover designed, subtitles added, a fact-check, a worksheet, a quiz, a template, a tool.

### The rule for studio content

- It must be **free to watch or use**, publicly available, with no login wall or paywall.
- It must **stay available**: it is not deleted or moved behind a paywall later. Exceptions: corrections, legal takedowns, or content a creator removes for safety or personal reasons (the studio keeps its credits and the learning material, e.g. scripts and worksheets).
- Written materials made by the studio (scripts, worksheets, quizzes, templates, guides) are published under **Creative Commons BY-NC-SA 4.0**: anyone may share and adapt them for free, with credit, but no one may sell them.
- Production assets in the [open asset library](16-open-asset-library.md) (music, footage, fonts, templates, diagrams) use **CC BY 4.0** (or CC0), so every creator can use them in videos, including monetised ones, with credit.
- Software made by the studio is **open source** (licence being finalised).

### The studio itself

- The studio is **non-profit, always**. It charges no fees, takes no revenue share, sells no products, runs no ads.
- Running costs (tools, hosting, AI usage) are covered by the founder, donations or education grants. Any funding we accept comes with no say over content.
- **Paid experts:** the studio may pay vetted experts for skills volunteers can't cover. It passes money through at their public rate and takes no fee or commission. See [09-paid-experts.md](09-paid-experts.md).
- **Supporting the team:** viewers can thank and support the people behind each video. Money given to a video's team goes only to its contributors and creators, never to the studio ([19-supporting-the-team.md](19-supporting-the-team.md)).
- **Public ledger:** every rupee that comes in or goes out, and every budget given to a person or system, is published in a ledger anyone can read. No money is accepted before the ledger is public. Full policy: [08-public-ledger.md](08-public-ledger.md).

## 2. What creators can and cannot do

Creators are independent. The studio does not limit how they earn a living, only how studio content is used.

| Allowed | Not allowed |
| --- | --- |
| Paid classes, tuition, workshops and work sessions they run themselves | Using volunteer hours to make paid products or paid-class material |
| Platform ad revenue and sponsorships on their own channels, as long as the studio content stays free to watch | Putting studio content behind a paywall, a paid course or a members-only tier |
| Mentioning their paid classes in their own posts | Promoting paid products inside studio community spaces |
| Leaving the studio at any time and keeping their channel and videos | Removing contributor credits from past studio videos |
| Rejecting any contributor's work | Making contributors do unpaid work that isn't education content |

### Pay it forward (a polite request, never a requirement)

The studio helped you for free. If you ever grow big, we kindly ask you to help the next teacher the same way.

- **Optional, always.** No contract, no pledge form, no deadline, no follow-up. Nothing you receive from the studio depends on it, now or later.
- **Only when you're well established.** The request applies only once your income from content (ads, brand deals, sponsorships, anything else) passes a comfortable threshold. Below that, please don't give anything.
- **A suggested amount, not a fee.** Around 10% of income *above* the threshold, whatever feels right to you.
- **Time counts as much as money.** Mentor new creators, run free workshops or collabs, or pledge hours to the studio.
- **Seen and thanked.** Anything you give is recorded in the [public ledger](08-public-ledger.md) and goes to a budget line for supporting new creators. With your permission, we thank you publicly.
- **No checks.** We never ask about your income, and there is no audit.

### Sponsored content

Studio help may be used on a sponsored piece only if the sponsor is education-safe, has no say over the educational claims, and the sponsorship is clearly disclosed.

## 3. Crediting standard

Every studio-supported video carries a credit block in the caption or description:

```
Made with Open Education Studio
Script review: <name> · Fact-check: <name>
Edit: <name> · Cover: <name> · Subtitles: <name>
AI use: <each AI-assisted part, and who checked it> (or "none")
Meet the team behind this video: <studio link>
```

- Contributors can choose their real name or a handle.
- Tag contributors where the platform allows (Instagram collab or @mention, YouTube description).
- Long videos also carry an end card.
- AI help is always disclosed in the "AI use" line (see section 5).

## 4. Corrections: owning our mistakes

Every creator who works with the studio accepts this policy. It applies to all studio content, and to the creator's own claims in it.

### When a correction is needed

Anyone (a viewer, a contributor, a teacher, a scientist) can report a possible error. The subject-expert panel checks it within 7 days against reliable sources.

| Level | Example | What the creator does | Deadline after confirmation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Minor** | A wrong date, a misspelt name, a number slightly off with no effect on the lesson | Pinned comment + corrected caption/description | 3 days |
| **Significant** | A wrong explanation, a misleading number, a claim that is outdated or disputed | **Correction video** + pinned comment + caption note + fixed version if possible | 14 days |
| **Serious** | A claim that could harm health or safety, or that spreads widely and misleads | Take the video down or re-upload a fixed version **immediately**, then a **correction video** | 48 hours |

### What a correction video includes

1. What we said, shown plainly.
2. Why it was wrong, with the evidence.
3. What is actually true, taught properly.
4. **Credit to whoever caught it**, by name or handle if they agree ("Thank you to Dr. X / @viewer for pointing this out").
5. What we changed (the updated video, caption or description).

It is framed as a lesson, not an apology tour: "Here's how science corrects itself, and here's what we learned."

### Everywhere else
- The original video's caption gets a note at the top: "Correction (date): … See the correction video: link".
- The studio keeps a **public corrections log**: date, video, what was wrong, level, who reported it, link to the correction.
- Contributors who worked on the video are told what went wrong so checklists can improve. The goal is learning, not blame.

### If a creator refuses
The studio pauses support for that creator until the correction is made, publishes its own note in the corrections log, and removes the studio credit from the video.

### New evidence, not mistakes
Science changes. When new research changes an answer that was right at the time, the creator adds an update note ("New research in 2027 changed this") and, for important topics, makes an update video. This is labelled an **update**, not a correction.

## 5. AI transparency

AI is a welcome tool, but viewers always have the right to know what a machine made. Every creator who works with the studio must disclose AI use, publicly, every time.

### A public AI-use statement for every creator

Each creator publishes a short statement on their studio profile and link-in-bio page: which AI tools they use and for which parts of their work (e.g. "I use AI to draft scripts and subtitles; every script is fact-checked by a person; I never use AI voices"). It is updated whenever their practice changes.

### A disclosure on every piece

Every studio-supported piece lists each part AI touched, as part of the credit block:

```
AI use: script first draft (rewritten by Rohail, fact-checked by Bilal R.)
        · thumbnail background (AI-generated) · subtitles (AI draft, checked by Hamza A.)
```

If no AI was used: `AI use: none`.

| Part of the work | Must be disclosed if AI was used for |
| --- | --- |
| Research and script | Drafting, outlining, rewriting, translating |
| Voice | Any synthetic or cloned voice, including dubs |
| Visuals | Generated images, video, animation, avatars, diagrams |
| Thumbnail / cover | Generated or heavily AI-edited images |
| Subtitles and translation | Drafting or checking |
| Music and sound | Generated tracks or effects |

### On screen, where it matters most

- A visible **"AI" label** on screen whenever an AI-generated voice, a realistic AI image or AI video appears.
- The platform's own AI label (YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, Facebook) is switched on whenever the platform requires it.
- In documentary and news-style content, AI-generated images are never shown as if they were real footage, even with a label.

### Never
- Deepfakes of real people, or cloning anyone's voice or face without their written consent.
- Presenting AI output as a real photo, recording, experiment result or expert statement.
- Publishing any AI-made claim that a named person hasn't fact-checked (section 4).

### How it's tracked
Every step in the [content workflow](15-content-workflow.md) records whether AI was used and which tool, so the disclosure line is generated from the record, just like credits. The QA check blocks publishing if the disclosure is missing.

### If a disclosure is missing
- Forgotten: add it within 3 days, logged in the corrections log.
- Hidden on purpose: studio support is paused and the studio publishes a note, as with a refused correction.

## 6. Rules for creators

1. Make education your core. Entertainment is welcome when it teaches.
2. Credit every contributor on every studio-supported piece, including after you leave.
3. Keep studio content free and available (section 1).
4. Every script is fact-checked before shooting. You have the final say on everything published.
5. Own your mistakes: when an error is confirmed, correct it publicly as set out in section 4, including a correction video for significant errors.
6. **Disclose AI use:** keep your public AI-use statement current and disclose every AI-assisted part of every piece (section 5).
7. **Urdu first:** every studio-supported piece must have an Urdu version. English terms are fine inside Urdu content; English or regional-language versions are welcome in addition, never instead. (See the [language policy](11-content-strategy.md#language-policy-urdu-first).)
8. Give feedback on contributors' work within 3 days, even if it is one line.
9. Never share account passwords with contributors. You post; the studio prepares.
10. Get consent before filming anyone, and a parent's or school's consent for anyone under 18.

## 7. Rules for contributors

1. Pledge honestly and pause when life gets busy; there is no penalty for pausing.
2. Accept only tasks you can finish by the deadline; hand back early if you can't.
3. Follow the checklist for your role.
4. Keep unreleased work private until the creator publishes it.
5. Cite sources for every fact you add.
6. Give the creator the right to use your work; you keep the right to show it in your portfolio.
7. Be kind in reviews: say what works, then what to fix.

## 8. Community conduct

- Education only. No selling, recruiting or self-promotion in studio spaces (a #portfolio channel is the one exception).
- No hate, harassment, or sectarian or party-political content.
- Disagree about facts with sources, not with people.
- Moderators may remove content and pause members; repeated or serious breaches mean removal.

## 9. How decisions are made

- **Core team** (founder and discipline leads) owns the charter and the roadmap.
- **Creators** decide everything about their own content.
- **Charter changes** are proposed in public, open for comment for 14 days, then decided by the core team and logged in this file.
