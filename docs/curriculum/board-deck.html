Curriculum board briefing

Software engineering education in the AI era

Keep coding as a literacy. Move systems knowledge and engineering judgement to the centre. Prove both with a record employers can verify.

25 October 2026

The shift

Every abstraction shift moves the value upward

Assembly to compilers

Hand-tuning machine code stopped being the price of entry. The work moved up a level, and almost nobody reads assembly now.

Painting to photography

Composition and light kept their value. Brush control did not. A new craft formed around deciding what image to make.

Code to language

The scarce skills become specifying what to build, choosing an approach and defending it, and verifying the result.

2 · Why change

What to teach

Six principles decide every course

Coding stays, as literacy

Year one is hand-written and unassisted. You cannot judge code you never struggled to write.

Judgement is the core

The defensible skill is the whiteboard defence: why this design, and what breaks if it is wrong.

Specs outrank code

Tests prove only that code matches the spec. A wrong spec passes every test.

Depth over coverage

Fewer topics, each to production standard. Memorised terms reproduce the problem we have.

Categories, not brands

Teach caching, not one vendor. Brands are the examples; they are never the syllabus.

Assemble before you build

Use battle-tested libraries for solved problems, and know why hand-rolling auth is a defect.

3 · What to teach

One generalist degree, with specialization as a spine

Not two or three swapped courses in the final year, which leaves no room for prerequisites.

Core CS

Semesters 1–8 · fundamentals, systems, networks, data, security, cloud

Track

Semesters 3–8 · one track course per semester, two in year 4

Practice

Semesters 1–8 · workshops, build-your-own projects, one long product project

General education

Semesters 1–2 · repurposed

Communication becomes technical writing; mathematics leans discrete, linear algebra and statistics; accounting becomes software economics.

4 · What to teach

Every topic is taught in the same five parts

This is what separates knowing a term from being able to use it.

01

The concept

What it is and what problem it solves, at the category level.

02

Good and bad

Two artefacts side by side, with the reasoning behind each.

03

How it fails

The production failure mode, not the happy path.

04

The caveats

Every technology’s weak side, stated plainly.

05

The lab

The student breaks it, fixes it, or defends the choice.

The habit is the transferable part. A student who knows one database’s limitations will interrogate the next technology the same way, including ones that do not exist yet.

5 · What to teach

How to run it

Theory becomes automatic only through repetition

Workshops

Twenty to fifty over four years. At most five concepts each, a fixed assessment, and a score.

Build-your-own projects

An HTTP server, a small database, a job queue. Fifty to a hundred on the list, graded by tests.

One long product project

Carried across semesters, handed out as tickets. Branching, review, release, handover.

GitHub from semester 1

All coursework in it. Count tests passed and projects finished, never commit streaks.

6 · How to run it

Assess what cannot be outsourced

AI makes take-home work trivial to fake. Diagnosis, review and defence are not.

Break-fix drills

A working system with a fault planted in it. Diagnose and fix under time pressure, repeatedly, until it is reflex.

Reviewing AI code

Pull requests with planted defects. Finding the problem is the graded skill, not writing the code.

Whiteboard defence

The central exam. Justify the design, and challenge the parts that are wrong.

No-AI workshops

Assessments run unassisted, though diagrams and architecture views are allowed, as they are in real work.

9 · How to run it · figures as published 25 Oct 2026

The main risk

Most faculty have not run production systems

A curriculum that depends on the instructor being the expert will not survive the timetable. Three measures remove that dependency.

Expertise in the materials

Every scenario ships with its answer key, its reasoning and its marking criteria. The instructor assesses against a rubric.

Practitioners in the labs

Working engineers, including remote contributors, run the practical sessions. Faculty carry the theory they already teach well.

Train faculty first

A summer cohort takes the same workshops and the same assessments before the students start.

10 · How to run it

The ask

Approve one pilot elective, and judge it on evidence

Roughly thirty students. Faculty training cohort first, then the elective, then this board reviews results before anything reaches the core degree.

Measured on

  • Break-fix scores, start against end
  • Whiteboard defence marks
  • Workshops passed per student
  • Projects completed with tests passing
  • Placement against a comparison group

Costs this semester

  • No new budget line
  • Cloud and AI from free education programmes
  • Project list and autograding on existing tools
  • One elective slot and one instructor
  • Faculty time for the summer cohort
